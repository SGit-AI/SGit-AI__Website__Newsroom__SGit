£10, a commercial licence, and a name on the review

2026-09-16 · Voice memo, transcribed — arrived twice, as a raw transcript and a cleaned one. The cleaned copy is below; the two differ only in punctuation and disfluency, and nothing in the substance moved.

The entry level goes to £10 and gains a commercial licence over the CC BY version anybody can download. And the two upper levels get a named reviewer, starting with the project lead, on a surface built for several.

What we read in it

The price rise is not the change. The licence is

Two reviewers said £5 reads as a commodity — a human partner and an invented buyer who had never met them. A larger number for the same artefact answers neither. What changes is what is bought: the public download is CC BY and carries an attribution obligation, and the buyer gets a commercial licence to use that vault however they want. The free templates and the paid pack stop being the same thing at two prices, which is the actual answer to the commodity reading.

It is grantable because the copyright is ours

Stated in the memo and it is the load-bearing fact. Everything in the pack was written here, so a second licence over the same bytes is ours to give. A pack containing third-party material could not carry this offer, which makes it a constraint on what goes in a pack from now on.

The licensee is unnamed on purpose

“A commercial licence to the buyer… I don’t think we need to name them.” That is the part worth research rather than drafting: a grant to an unnamed holder has to read as a grant and not as a public re-licence of the same bytes, or it gives away what it is selling.

A named reviewer is the answer to a real hole

The two upper levels are somebody’s work and the store has never said whose. Asking for £1,500 for a security review from nobody in particular is a worse problem than any price. The memo also says what to build for: a list, not a person — “for now you only have one, which is me, but we will have more soon.”

And the buyer chooses

“When somebody purchases something, they should be choosing who they want to do the assessment.” With one reviewer that is a statement rather than a choice, and it should still be on the page, because what it says is that there is a name behind the work.

What this does not decide

The licence wording, and what it travels with

Does the grant attach to the file, or to the order reference? What happens on resale? Does it survive the buyer’s company changing name? None of this is drafted and none of it should be guessed at on a selling page.

The reviewer’s own page

Nothing goes on it that is not traceable to a published source. The memo names the open-source site, sgit.ai and a LinkedIn profile the project lead will supply. A biography this site invented would be the worst possible thing to stand next to a £1,500 price.
