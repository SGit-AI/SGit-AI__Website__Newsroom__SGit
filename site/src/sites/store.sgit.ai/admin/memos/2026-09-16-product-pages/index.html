Make a product page feel like a product page

2026-09-16 · Voice memo, transcribed

Description, price and buy button, a carousel of screenshots of what you actually get, specs and SKUs, and a place for reviews. Built as prototypes first, then wired in — and eventually switchable by audience, the way a product has editions.

What we read in it

The diagnosis is blunt and it is right

“We go there, we don’t know what we’re buying.” A level page on this store argues about what a policy is. A product page shows the thing. Those are different documents and the store has only ever written the first one.

Screenshots are the substance, not decoration

“Screenshots of the vault, screenshots of the grant, the mandate… what does the board version of the guidance look like.” Every one of those exists already — the synthetic run alone produced 43, and the review pages embed a live vault. What is missing is a place on a selling page to put them.

Specs and SKUs, because a product has them

The store has 62 of them and prints none. Stripe is about to need the catalogue anyway, so the spec block and the rail's product list are the same data.

Reviews, with the emptiness admitted

“There’s no product on Amazon that doesn’t have stars and reviews.” The memo asks for the placeholder now and says the reviews are coming, given away in exchange for comment. An empty review section that says it is empty is this site’s house style — it is what the two upper levels already do about never having been sold.

Editions, which is the five-audience memo arriving from the other side

“Do you want this for the startup founder? Do you want the investor version?” Framed here as a product variant rather than a separate path — same switch, same data, on the product page instead of at the front door. The two memos want one mechanism.

Prototype first, and there is already a place for that

“Start by creating prototypes on the side, then we review it, then we wire it up.” /lab/ is exactly that surface: five prototypes of one flow, every one of them stating on its own face that nothing there can be bought. A prototype product page belongs there and inherits that protection for free.

What this does not decide

A prototype that looks like a shop is the most dangerous page here

The lab exists because a page that looks like a checkout and is not one is the worst thing this site could publish, and a build check fails the release if a lab page stops saying so. An Amazon-style prototype is the closest anything has come to that line.

How many screenshots, and whose

“Tons and tons.” They have to be current, or the product page becomes the place the store goes stale first. The walkthrough PDFs already carry a version in their filename for exactly this reason.

Where the product media lives

The memo says it should eventually come from riskmandate.ai, and the ownership memo says the same. Building it here first is explicitly allowed; it is debt, and it needs marking as debt.

Stars

Not asked for. A rating out of five on a product nobody has bought would be the one piece of furniture on this page that could not be honest.
