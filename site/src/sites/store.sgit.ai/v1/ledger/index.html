What the states mean

Four of the ten states are ways of saying "not yet", and that proportion is the point rather than an embarrassment.

StateWhat it means
exists and runsBuilt, and it runs today
measuredMeasured by our own pipeline, on a named workload and date
read, not runRead from a published source on this date. Never executed by us
projectedArithmetic, not an invoice. The workings are shown
specified, not builtA specification. The thing it specifies does not exist
delivered by a personA named security professional does this work and signs it off. It is somebody's time rather than a pipeline, which is why its delivery estimate depends on a calendar
built, not locatedIt was built. It has not been found since, and nothing here promises it until it is
a booking, not a downloadWhat is bought is a person's time, not a file
part existsOne half runs. The half carrying the guarantee does not
does not exist yetIt does not exist. It is listed so nobody proposes it as new

Why a store publishes one of these

Because checkability is the thing being sold. An offer page that cannot be checked is arguing against the product sitting beside it.

And because the alternative is worse in a specific, predictable way: without a ledger, the sentence "the pipeline does not exist" survives one draft, gets softened to "the pipeline is in development" in the next, and is gone by the third. Here it is a row with a state, cited from the page that makes the claim, and **the build fails if a page cites a claim that is not in this table.**
