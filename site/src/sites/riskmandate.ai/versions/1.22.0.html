The home page sells the Agent Behaviour Policy, and holds no price

The synthetic-user study ↗ found that the home page never said what the product was called. This is the answer to it, measured against the same numbers so the two can be compared.

The vision is where it was. Make your agents insurable is still the headline, and the sub now turns immediately: *that is the destination, and you cannot insure what nobody can describe — so the thing we make and sell today is an Agent Behaviour Policy.* The hero card is captioned **Agent Behaviour Policy · template** rather than A real policy, which was the phrase two readers took for an insurance policy.

Two new sections, and they are the page now. What an Agent Behaviour Policy is↗ — the four objects with the verb on each, and the note that it carries no score. What it delivers↗ — a vault rather than a PDF: the files your agent reads, the policy and its workings, a mandate you correct, and a licence to operate that ships unissued.

How to get one↗ puts the free line first. Fifteen shapes published in full with read keys, then the four levels, each linking to the store. What the paid levels add is a commercial licence over the same material and, at the top two, a named professional doing the correction.

This site no longer holds a price, anywhere. The store published the boundary ↗ on 16 September: the store owns the cart, the order reference, the prices and the page after payment; riskmandate.ai owns the shapes, the policies, the vaults and the explanation, and never runs a cart, holds a price or issues an order reference. Every price on this site is gone, and the pages that named a level by its price now name it by its level.

A correction that came with it. The entry level is £10 and this site was saying £5 in six places, including a generator that wrote it onto eighteen vault pages. That was true when it was written and stopped being true. It is fixed at source. The one £5 that stays is on the brief register ↗, where it is a dated record of what the store's prices were on 15 September and changing it would be rewriting history rather than correcting it.

Moved rather than deleted: Two things changed, and neither of them was us — the 2026 exclusion and the regulator's assignment of responsibility — is now on the insurance page ↗, where the rest of that argument already lives. Two personas skipped it on the home page, and both said why: it is an insurance argument and they had not come about insurance. It is two screens shorter here and in the right place there.
