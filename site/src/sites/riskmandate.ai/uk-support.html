UK support, in the open

Every UK programme, event, scheme and network a London startup could use to get in front of users, read on the official page and dated, with what RiskMandate has done about each one. Written to be forwarded and corrected.

Source: https://riskmandate.ai/uk-support.html↗

A UK startup with a product. Now it needs users.

RiskMandate is a London company, and the product is ready. What we need now is traction: people using it, rooms to show it in, and introductions to the people who would buy it. The UK has a great deal of support for companies at exactly this point, spread across departments, agencies, trade bodies, banks and conferences. This page is every door we found, read on the official page and dated, with what we have done about each one, including nothing.

One page, three readers.

The same research serves three people, so it is written once and published rather than kept in a notebook. Nothing on it would need to be confidential on either side, because all of it is on the programmes' own pages.

Am I missing something?

This page is written to be forwarded. If you know a programme, an event, a person in a department or a room of risk managers that should be on it, the most useful thing you can send is its name and its link.

Take it.

Most of this applies to any UK startup with a product and not enough users. The register is also published as data, uk-support.json↗, so you can copy it, filter it and keep your own status column.

What we did, and what happened.

Each row carries our own status. When we apply, speak, get turned down or get in, the row says so, with the date. A founder reading it later sees what the door was actually like to walk through, in facts rather than opinions.

The product is on sale. What we need is users.

Sixteen template Agent Behaviour Policies are published as encrypted vaults with their read keys, the thirteen prompts are free, and the first three levels can be bought from the store today; the fourth is waiting on its payment link. We exhibited at Startup Summit Lisbon in September 2026 and are going to Web Summit in November. What we do not yet have is a steady stream of people using it, and every lesson we have learnt so far came from somebody using it.

Three things we did not expect, and why search results are out of date.

The departments moved.

The Department for Business and Trade became the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade. AI strategy, public-sector AI adoption and the AI Security Institute moved to the Cabinet Office, and DCMS now leads cyber security policy for the economy. Many pages, and most search results, still use the old names. gov.uk fact sheet ↗, read 24 September 2026.

The travel grant is gone.

The support founders remember, where the department paid towards a stand and the trip, was the UK Tradeshow Programme, withdrawn on 7 June 2023, and the Internationalisation Fund, closed in January 2023. What exists now is paid UK-branded pods, missions for companies already inside a government-backed programme, and an events listing. No grant towards exhibiting abroad was open on the day we read.

The two cyber programmes closed.

NCSC for Startups has ended and its page is an archive. Cyber Runway is closed with no next cohort published. The routes still open are conferences, the London cyber cluster, LASR for AI security, and accelerators run with insurers.

A government challenge for agents in specific contexts.

The Sovereign AI R&D Procurement Scheme, announced on 31 August 2026, buys R&D from UK startups in batches every two months. Its third challenge is set with the National Cyber Security Centre, and its competition guidance words it like this:

“Enabling safe AI agent adoption – Produce an evidence-based, operational, and ideally automated risk management approach for CISOs to support risk-based decision-making for specific agents in specific contexts. The risk management approach should include both resilience and cyber security risks. … Solutions that support wide-scale adoption, such as those built on open-source frameworks, will be preferred.”

Competition guidance, Sovereign AI R&D Procurement Scheme, linked from sovereignai.gov.uk ↗, read 24 September 2026. Contracts of £250,000 to £10 million. Batch deadlines 1 October 2026, 1 December 2026 and 1 February 2027; the expression of interest is due at least two weeks before. An Agent Behaviour Policy is one agent in one deployment, and the toolkit is open source. We have not applied yet. Whether to is the lead’s decision, and this row will say what it was.

Two chips on each row. The first is what the programme’s own page said on the day it was read: open, rolling, a date, closed, or unclear when the page refused our reader or said nothing. Government money, advice and government as first customer.

The Department for Business and Trade became the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade on 22 July 2026, and AI strategy and the AI Security Institute moved to the Cabinet Office. Some pages still carry the old names. Where a row says the department, it means the renamed one.

Sovereign AI R&D Procurement Scheme ↗

R&D contracts of £250,000 to £10 million, most expected at £1 million to £3 million. The supplier keeps its IP. No minimum turnover or trading history.

Who it is for. UK-registered startups and SMEs, project delivered in the UK. First join the Approved Supplier List through an expression of interest, at least two weeks before a batch deadline.

For us. Challenge 3, Enabling safe AI agent adoption, asks for a risk management approach for CISOs "for specific agents in specific contexts". That is what an Agent Behaviour Policy is. It would also make government the first customer, on systems we would be contracted to run.

Next. Expression of interest by about 17 November 2026 for the 1 December batch; batch 3 is 1 February 2027. Guidance PDF linked from the site, read 24 September 2026.

Sovereign AI Fund ↗

A £500 million fund writing £1 million to £10 million cheques. Trust, safety & assurance is one of its stated focus areas.

Who it is for. UK AI companies.

For us. A priced round later. Investee status confers no advantage in the procurement scheme above, and the guidance says so.

AI Assurance Innovation Fund ↗

£11 million for "novel AI assurance mechanisms", according to the roadmap of 3 September 2025.

Who it is for. Not stated.

For us. The subject matches directly. The roadmap said round one would open in spring 2026; the sector plan of 10 June 2026 says the fund was established; no call is visible on the Innovation Funding Service today.

Next. Ask the business support helpline which department runs it now.

Grants, loans and contracts, per competition. The forward list is on UKRI's future opportunities page.

Who it is for. Varies by competition.

For us. On 24 September 2026 none of the 26 open competitions was about AI assurance or cyber security, apart from the Singapore one below. Worth a monthly look.

Next. UK-Germany bilateral round 5 and two Frontier AI SME calls are listed as opening in October 2026.

For us. Only with a Singapore partner, such as an insurer or a company deploying agents.

Next. Closes 16 December 2026, 11am.

SEIS and EIS advance assurance ↗

HMRC's view that a share issue is likely to qualify for investor tax relief. SEIS allows up to £250,000 raised; EIS up to £10 million a year.

Who it is for. UK trading companies within the limits on each scheme's page. First-time applicants name prospective investors.

For us. Most UK angel cheques expect it, and HMRC states no turnaround time, so it is started before a raise, not during one.

R&D tax relief, merged scheme and ERIS ↗

A 20% credit under the merged scheme; loss-making companies spending at least 30% on R&D can claim enhanced support.

Who it is for. Companies doing qualifying R&D.

For us. Engineering spend may qualify. Whether this work counts as R&D needs a specialist's view, not ours.

Start Up Loans ↗

A personal loan of £500 to £25,000 over one to five years, with up to twelve months of mentoring.

Who it is for. UK residents over 18 whose business has traded for less than five years.

For us. It is a personal loan to a founder, not company money.

Help to Grow: Management ↗

A twelve-week course, 90% funded, £750 to the company.

Who it is for. SMEs with 5 to 249 employees.

For us. Not until the team has five people.

Next. King's College London cohort starts 9 November 2026.

Global Business Innovation Programme ↗

Group flights, accommodation and in-market travel for one senior person on an innovation mission; a £1,000 commitment fee refunded on completion.

Who it is for. UK SMEs.

For us. This is the closest current thing to the government-sponsored trips founders describe. No AI or cyber mission is listed today.
