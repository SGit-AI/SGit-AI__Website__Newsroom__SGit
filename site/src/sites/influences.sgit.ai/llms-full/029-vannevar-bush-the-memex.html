Vannevar Bush & the memex

The ancestor of the whole external-memory idea, and it entered this register the long way round — by being cited in an article about a modern implementation of the thing it predicted.

Block 1 — The anchor

As We May Think — Vannevar Bush — The Atlantic · 1945

  • anchor: https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/1945/07/as-we-may-think/303881/ ↗

Linked, never rehosted.

Block 2 — In his own words

Bush's specific idea was not storage. It was the trail: a named path through documents that one person builds and another can follow, which is a claim about edges being authored rather than derived. Eighty years on, that is still not what most systems do.

The loop is worth naming out loud, and so is its weakness. This site network's own thesis is external memory for agents; Luhmann ↗ is the paper version arrived at independently; the Semantic Web ↗ is the standards-body version. Bush is upstream of all of it — and the evidence that he shaped this work is one citation inside an article about something else. A satisfying lineage is not the same as a documented one, which is why this is a stub and not a full entry.

Block 3 — The principle

The mind works by association, and an external memory should too — trails between documents, made by a person and followable by anyone afterwards.
