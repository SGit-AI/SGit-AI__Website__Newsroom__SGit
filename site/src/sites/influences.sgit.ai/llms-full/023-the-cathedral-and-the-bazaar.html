The Cathedral and the Bazaar

The essay's factual history belongs to the open-source sibling site. This entry owns only what it did to Dinis Cruz — which is exactly the part that is not written down anywhere.

Block 1 — The anchor

The Cathedral and the Bazaar — Eric S. Raymond — 1997

  • anchor: http://www.catb.org/~esr/writings/cathedral-bazaar/ ↗

Published free by its author and still online at its original address, which is itself part of the argument.

Linked, never rehosted.

Block 2 — In his own words

The deconfliction here is unusually sharp. open-source.sgit.ai↗ carries the essay's history, its influence on the movement and the arguments it started. If this entry repeated any of that it would be duplicating a sibling, so it is deliberately empty of everything except the one thing that sibling cannot hold: what reading it in the late nineties or early two-thousands did to a particular person's idea of how software gets made. That is Dinis Cruz's to write.

Block 3 — The principle

Release early, release often, and let the people using the thing find what is wrong with it — openness as a development method rather than a licensing choice.

Block 7 — The wider library

  • The Cathedral and the Bazaar (1997) — the essay, free at its original address http://www.catb.org/~esr/writings/cathedral-bazaar/ ↗
  • Homesteading the Noosphere (1998) — the follow-up on ownership and reputation in open-source projects http://www.catb.org/~esr/writings/homesteading/ ↗
  • Two Bits — Christopher Kelty (2008) — not by Raymond, and the best critical account of what the essay got right and wrong https://twobits.net/ ↗

Awaiting the briefing document

Named by Dinis Cruz. The corpus covers the essay's factual history through the open-source material and says nothing about its personal effect — and the personal effect is the whole content of this entry.

Research plan. Dinis Cruz's briefing, N4 ↗.
