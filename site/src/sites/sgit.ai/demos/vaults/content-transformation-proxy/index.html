Content-Transformation Proxy

"The proxy that shows you only what your policy allows." A request passes through mitmproxy, a service injects a transformation script, and the page is rewritten before the user sees it. This vault is the as-built write-up, three parts, ten authored slides, a diagram library and the original source decks, published as one navigable documentation site.

What is in it

the shape

A dev brief that reads like a site, not a folder of PDFs

Nine sections across the top (Overview, Architecture, Vault, Deployment, Slides, Diagrams, Decks, Gallery, Sources) over material from a three-part dev brief dated 11 June 2026. Each part is written up in full with its slides and architecture diagrams placed inline, plus a deck viewer, a diagram library and a reader for the original PDFs.

Two deployment modes are worked through: an inline proxy, and a streamed remote browser where a container runs one browser per user and only pixels reach the client, "nothing to install, all you need is a browser."

The overview: the thesis, the flow through mitmproxy, and the as-built provenance line.

the point

Source material and finished explanation in the same object

The vault carries the rendered site, the ten authored infographics, the architecture diagrams, and the source PDFs the write-up came from. A reader who doubts the summary can open what it was made from without asking anyone.

That is the property a document attachment loses the moment it is emailed: the explanation and its evidence stop travelling together. Here one read key moves both.

The vault browser: the rendered site, the diagram library and the source decks in one tree.
